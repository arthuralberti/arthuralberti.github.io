Reading list

Favorite Papers

A compact shelf of papers I keep returning to: political economy, growth, history, labor, macro, market design, and the craft of applied economics.

1
2024

Religion and Growth

Sascha O. Becker, Jared Rubin, and Ludger Woessmann

Journal of Economic Literature

Abstract Paper

This paper surveys how religion can affect economic growth through physical capital, human capital, labor supply, technology, institutions, political economy, and conflict. It organizes a broad literature using the logic of macroeconomic production and argues that religion can either support or constrain growth depending on the mechanisms at work.

2
2023

A Theory of Falling Growth and Rising Rents

Philippe Aghion, Antonin Bergeaud, Timo Boppart, Peter J. Klenow, and Huiyu Li

Review of Economic Studies

Abstract Paper

This paper proposes a theory linking slower growth, rising concentration, higher aggregate markups, and increased rents. Falling costs of operating across markets allow highly efficient firms to expand, temporarily raising productivity but making entry and innovation less attractive for other firms, which can reduce long-run growth.

3
2020

Productivity and Misallocation in General Equilibrium

David Rezza Baqaee and Emmanuel Farhi

Quarterly Journal of Economics

Abstract Paper

This paper develops a general-equilibrium framework for measuring how microeconomic distortions affect aggregate productivity when firms and sectors are connected through input-output linkages. It shows that misallocation depends on network structure, complementarities, and nonlinear interactions, so aggregate productivity losses cannot be inferred from micro wedges in isolation.

4
2017

The Economist as Plumber

Esther Duflo

American Economic Review

Abstract Paper

This paper argues that economists should treat implementation as a central part of policy design rather than as an afterthought. By comparing economists to plumbers, it emphasizes diagnosis, iteration, institutional detail, and feedback in the design of policies that work in practice.

5
2017

Human Capital Persistence and Development

Rudi Rocha, Claudio Ferraz, and Rodrigo R. Soares

American Economic Journal: Applied Economics

Abstract Paper

This paper examines long-run human-capital persistence using a Brazilian settlement policy that attracted more educated immigrants to selected regions in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. A century later, treated municipalities had higher schooling and income, with persistence operating through educational inputs and occupational upgrading.

6
2015

Voting Technology, Political Responsiveness, and Infant Health: Evidence from Brazil

Thomas Fujiwara

Econometrica

Abstract Paper

This paper studies the introduction of electronic voting in Brazil and shows that the technology reduced residual votes among less educated citizens, effectively expanding political participation. The resulting enfranchisement shifted policy toward public health spending and improved infant health outcomes, linking voting technology to political responsiveness and development.

7
2012

Vote-Buying and Reciprocity

Frederico Finan and Laura A. Schechter

Econometrica

Abstract Paper

This paper examines vote buying in Paraguay and argues that its effectiveness depends on reciprocity rather than only monitoring or coercion. Voters with stronger reciprocal preferences are more likely to be targeted by brokers and more likely to vote for the party that offered benefits, suggesting that vote buying can operate through internalized social obligations.

8
2007

'Whatever Is, Is Right'? Economic Institutions in Pre-industrial Europe

Sheilagh Ogilvie

Economic History Review

Abstract Paper

This paper critiques the view that pre-industrial European institutions should be interpreted mainly as efficient responses to economic problems. It argues that many institutions persisted because they distributed rents, reinforced power, and excluded outsiders, so institutional analysis must account for distributional conflict as well as efficiency.

9
2002

Factor Endowments, Inequality, and Paths of Development among New World Economies

Stanley L. Engerman and Kenneth L. Sokoloff

Economia

Abstract Paper

This paper argues that initial factor endowments in the New World shaped inequality, political power, institutions, and public policies. Economies with conditions conducive to plantation agriculture and slavery developed more unequal societies and less inclusive institutions, helping explain divergent long-run development paths across the Americas.

10
2002

Organizing Violence

Robert H. Bates, Avner Greif, and Smita Singh

Journal of Conflict Resolution

Abstract Paper

This paper analyzes violence as an organized activity and studies how specialists in coercion can either protect production or prey upon it. The framework connects political order, economic development, and the institutional arrangements that channel the incentives of those capable of using force.

11
2002

The Economist as Engineer: Game Theory, Experimentation, and Computation as Tools for Design Economics

Alvin E. Roth

Econometrica

Abstract Paper

This paper presents market design as a form of engineering in which game theory, experiments, computation, and institutional detail are used to build real allocation mechanisms. Drawing on matching markets, it explains how economists can move from analysis to practical design.

12
2000

Economics and Identity

George A. Akerlof and Rachel E. Kranton

Quarterly Journal of Economics

Abstract Paper

This paper incorporates identity into economic utility by allowing behavior to depend on social categories and the prescriptions attached to them. The framework shows how identity can change incentives and outcomes in settings such as gender, work, education, and social interaction, expanding standard models of preferences.

13
1995

Money and Prices: A Model of Search and Bargaining

Shouyong Shi

Journal of Economic Theory

Abstract Paper

This paper introduces divisible commodities and bargaining into a search-theoretic model of money in order to determine the purchasing power of fiat money endogenously. It shows that multiple monetary equilibria can coexist, with different implications for prices, output, welfare, velocity, currency substitution, and exchange-market intervention.

14
1994

Minimum Wages and Employment: A Case Study of the Fast-Food Industry in New Jersey and Pennsylvania

David Card and Alan B. Krueger

American Economic Review

Abstract Paper

This paper studies New Jersey's 1992 minimum wage increase by comparing fast-food restaurants in New Jersey with restaurants in neighboring Pennsylvania before and after the policy change. The evidence does not show the employment decline predicted by the competitive model and became a central reference point for empirical minimum-wage research.

15
1993

Discretion versus Policy Rules in Practice

John B. Taylor

Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series on Public Policy

Abstract Paper

This paper compares discretionary monetary policy with policy rules and proposes a simple interest-rate rule that describes how the federal funds rate can respond to inflation and real activity. It evaluates the practical performance of such rules across historical episodes and argues that systematic policy can improve macroeconomic outcomes.

16
1989

Fertility Choice in a Model of Economic Growth

Robert J. Barro and Gary S. Becker

Econometrica

Abstract Paper

This paper builds a growth model in which fertility is chosen endogenously by altruistic families. The model links child quantity, intergenerational transfers, human welfare, and capital accumulation, showing how demographic choices can interact with long-run economic growth.

17
1989

Reputation and Coalitions in Medieval Trade: Evidence on the Maghribi Traders

Avner Greif

Journal of Economic History

Abstract Paper

This paper uses evidence on Maghribi traders to study how reputation and coalition-based enforcement supported long-distance trade in the medieval Mediterranean. It shows how repeated relationships, information sharing, and collective punishment could sustain agency relations when formal legal enforcement was limited.

18
1988

Fairness and Unemployment

George A. Akerlof and Janet L. Yellen

American Economic Review

Abstract Paper

This paper develops the idea that fairness considerations can affect wage setting and unemployment. If workers compare actual wages with perceived fair wages and adjust effort accordingly, firms may choose wage policies that depart from market-clearing levels, producing involuntary unemployment and wage rigidity.
